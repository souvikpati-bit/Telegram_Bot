This week I want to talk about the word "clean", because I get asked about it more than almost anything else.

The first thing to know is that it has no regulatory definition. There is no standard a product has to meet to call itself clean, so every brand that uses the word is using its own list of what it has chosen to leave out. The second thing is that those lists are usually built around ingredients that sound alarming rather than ingredients that have been shown to cause problems at the levels used. The third is that removing an ingredient always means replacing its job with something else, and that something else is rarely discussed.

Preservatives are the clearest example. A water-based product without an effective preservative system is not purer. It is a product that can grow things you would never knowingly put on your face.

I'm not trying to make you distrust every product on your shelf, and I'm not saying every "free-from" claim is wrong. Some people do react to particular ingredients, and avoiding them is sensible. I'm saying that what a formula leaves out tells you much less than how it was put together.

A better question than "is this clean?" is "what is doing the job of the thing you removed, and how do you know it works?" Any brand should be able to answer that. Not from us specifically. From anyone.
