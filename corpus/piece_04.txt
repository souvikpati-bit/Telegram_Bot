We have spent 14 months, and counting, trying to make a vitamin C serum I am willing to sell.

The hard part is not getting vitamin C into a bottle. It is keeping it working for as long as the carton says it will. The most studied form is also one of the least stable. It oxidises when it meets air, light and heat, and it needs a pH below 3.8 to work well, which is itself hard on sensitive skin. Every fix for one of those problems tends to make another one worse.

The versions we have made so far perform well in the lab for the first few months. Then the colour shifts and the active drops, and in Indian summer conditions that happens faster than I am comfortable with. I could put one of them on sale and date it conservatively. I have decided not to.

I want to be honest about what I don't know yet. It is possible we won't find a formula that holds up to our own standard, in which case we won't launch one. This is not a setup for a product launch.

What I would say to anyone buying vitamin C is simple. Look at the colour when you open it and as you use it, because darkening is oxidation you can see. And ask the brand how long the active stays within specification after opening, not only before.
