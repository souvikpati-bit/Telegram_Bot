Most of the questions I get about sunscreen are about which one to buy. I want to answer a different question: why sunscreen that works well in one country can disappoint in another.

The first reason is the UV index. Across much of India it sits high for a large part of the year, which means the difference between a sunscreen that is applied properly and one that is applied thinly matters more here, not less. The second reason is heat and sweat. A formula that feels fine in a temperate climate can feel heavy enough in Indian summer that people use far less of it than the label assumes, and the protection on the label is measured at a generous amount. The third reason is that the testing behind a claimed SPF is done in controlled conditions that don't include a commute through Mumbai traffic.

I'm not selling a sunscreen, and this is not a lead-up to one. I'm saying that the number on the tube describes the product under ideal conditions, and your protection depends on how much you use and how it holds up on your skin during your day.

The practical part is this. Choose the sunscreen you will actually apply generously, reapply it when you have been sweating, and if a brand makes a water or sweat resistance claim, ask how it was tested.
