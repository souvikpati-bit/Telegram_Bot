The niacinamide serum you're using probably has the ingredient listed at 5% or 10%. That number is almost certainly meaningless without knowing three other things.

The first thing is the pH of the finished product. Niacinamide is stable across a fairly wide range, but the formula around it usually is not, and a serum that sits too acidic can slowly convert a small amount of niacinamide into nicotinic acid. That is the ingredient behind a lot of the flushing and stinging people blame on niacinamide itself. The label percentage tells you nothing about which side of that line the product sits on.

The second thing is what the niacinamide is sitting next to. Some formulas pair it with ingredients that need a very different pH to work, and one of them ends up compromised. The third thing is the concentration that actually has evidence behind it for the result you want. For some claims the research is reasonably consistent at lower levels. For others it is thinner than the marketing suggests.

I am not making a case that niacinamide doesn't work. It is one of the better-studied ingredients on the shelf. I'm saying that a higher number on the front of the bottle is not the same thing as a better product, and it is sometimes a worse one.

If you want to know whether a niacinamide product is well made, ask the brand for the pH of the finished formula. A brand that has done the work will have that number. A brand that only has the percentage has told you the least useful part.
